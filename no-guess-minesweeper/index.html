No-guess minesweeper

A board checked for a path you can solve with logic.

What does no-guess mean?

An ordinary random board can force a 50/50. A no-guess board has a route from its opening to every safe tile using deductions. You can still lose to a wrong move or a misplaced flag.

In the browser demoAll four strait boards start with the no-guess rule enabled. The opening and its immediate neighbours are protected. This describes the browser demo’s current settings; the full app also has configurable grid rules.

Tap. Check. Sweep.

  1. Choose your opening. The game prepares the mine layout around that tap.
  2. The solver checks it. It uses revealed clues and deductions to test whether the safe tiles can be cleared.
  3. Play the checked board. Find a pattern, reveal what is safe, and repeat.

Why Veteran stops at 21%

The browser demo caps its strait boards at 21% mines, rounded down. That means 24 mines on Small Hands and 187 on Hormuz Closed. The cap balances generation time and difficulty; it is a design choice, not a mathematical limit on minesweeper.

Stuck does not always mean guessing.

Subtract proven flags from the numbers. Check overlapping groups and the whole-board mine count. A 1–2–1 can hide inside a 2–3–2. The visual pattern guide shows how.

No-guess does not mean every tile is decidable immediately. A useful clue may be on another part of the board.

Try it on the coastline.

Land changes which tiles touch a number, but diagonals still count. Start with Small Hands, then work up to Hormuz Closed.
